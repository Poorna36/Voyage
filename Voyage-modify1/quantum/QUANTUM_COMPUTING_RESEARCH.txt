2. WHERE QUANTUM TECHNOLOGY APPEARS IN THE BROADER RESEARCH CONTEXT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
While SIH_dump itself holds the classical/deep-learning engineering pipeline for
lunar image registration, quantum computing and quantum technologies are active,
officially recognized research priorities in the ISRO Space Applications Centre
(SAC) research basket (RESPOND 2025-2026) that originated these exact satellite
payload problem domains.

Specifically, ISRO SAC has defined the following formal Quantum Research Areas:

[A] RES-SAC-2025-023:
    "Quantum Enhanced Algorithms Design and Development for Remote Sensing Data Processing"
    - Primary Objective: Investigating how quantum computing can accelerate and
      enhance remote sensing image analysis, satellite payload data handling,
      and complex pattern recognition.
    - Scope:
      * Quantum Image Processing (QIMP) algorithms (quantum edge detection,
        filtering, image enhancement).
      * Quantum Machine Learning (QML) models for Earth Observation and planetary
        multispectral/hyperspectral imagery classification.
      * Hybrid Classical-Quantum algorithms running on near-term NISQ (Noisy
        Intermediate-Scale Quantum) devices combined with HPC infrastructure.
      * Benchmarking quantum speedup and quantum representation fidelity over
        classical remote sensing pipelines.

[B] RES-SAC-2025-014:
    "Mathematical modelling, coding and simulation of Quantum Antenna Element and Array"
    - Primary Objective: Exploration of quantum phenomena (superconducting circuits,
      quantum state coherence, photonics) for microwave and RF antenna arrays
      used on spaceborne payloads.
    - Scope:
      * Simulation of quantum antenna structures for next-generation space
        communications and deep-space sensing.
      * High-frequency quantum electrodynamic (QED) modeling.

[C] RES-SAC-2025-028:
    "SDN-Based Network with Post-Quantum Cryptography for Secure and Optimized Traffic Management"
    - Primary Objective: Securing space-ground links and satellite constellation
      telemetry against future quantum computing attacks (Shor's algorithm).
    - Scope:
      * Implementation of NIST-standardized Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC)
        (e.g., lattice-based cryptography, ML-KEM, ML-DSA).
      * Software-Defined Networking (SDN) traffic orchestration for secure space
        data delivery.

4. DEEP-DIVE: FORMULATION OF QUANTUM FEATURE MATCHING (QUBO & QAOA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In SIH_dump, the central challenge is matching a set of keypoints P = {p_i} from
Chandrayaan-2 (OHRC/TMC) with keypoints Q = {q_j} from LRO (NAC/WAC).

4.1 The Combinatorial Problem:
Finding optimal point-to-point correspondence under geometric deformation can be
framed as a Quadratic Assignment Problem (QAP) or Maximum Weight Independent Set
(MWIS), which is NP-hard.

4.2 Mapping to Ising / QUBO Hamiltonian:
Let binary variable x_{i,j} = 1 if point p_i in Image A matches point q_j in Image B,
and 0 otherwise.

Objective Function:
  min H(x) = - sum_{i,j} U(p_i, q_j) * x_{i,j} 
             - sum_{i,j, k,l} W((p_i, q_j), (p_k, q_l)) * x_{i,j} * x_{k,l}
             + lambda * Penalty_Terms(x)

Where:
- U(p_i, q_j) represents Unary Affinity (similarity of descriptors, e.g. RIFT/RoMa).
- W((p_i, q_j), (p_k, q_l)) represents Pairwise Structural Consistency:
    e.g., | ||p_i - p_k|| - ||q_j - q_l|| | < epsilon (distance preservation).
- Penalty_Terms enforces one-to-one matching constraints:
    sum_j x_{i,j} <= 1  for all i
    sum_i x_{i,j} <= 1  for all j

4.3 Execution on Quantum Hardware:
1. Quantum Annealing (D-Wave):
   The QUBO matrix Q is embedded onto the QPU's chimera/pegasus topology. The system
   tunnels through energy barriers to find the ground state corresponding to the
   global minimum correspondence error, bypassing local minima that trap classical
   greedy matchers.
2. Gate-Based Quantum Systems (IBM Quantum / Google Quantum AI):
   Solvable via QAOA (Quantum Approximate Optimization Algorithm), alternating
   between a problem Hamiltonian H_P and a mixer Hamiltonian H_M over p layers:
     |gamma, beta> = prod_{m=1}^p [ e^{-i beta_m H_M} e^{-i gamma_m H_P} ] |+>^N

5. HARDWARE CONSTRAINTS & NISQ-ERA REALITY CHECK
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Why hasn't the current SIH_dump repository adopted pure quantum algorithms yet?

1. Image Resolution vs Qubit Count:
   - Chandrayaan-2 OHRC images have resolutions down to 0.25 m/pixel (often
     tens of thousands of pixels per swath).
   - Encoding even a small 256x256 image tile directly using NEQR requires:
     log2(256 * 256) = 16 position qubits + 8 color/greyscale qubits = 24 qubits.
     However, performing multi-qubit coherent entangling operations across these
     qubits exceeds the coherence time and gate fidelity of current NISQ hardware.

2. State Preparation Bottleneck (Quantum I/O Problem):
   - Loading classical satellite imagery into quantum states requires an
     initialization routine whose circuit depth can scale as O(2^n) or O(N),
     erasing the theoretical quantum speedup unless data is heavily compressed
     or represented via specialized quantum random access memory (qRAM).

3. Hybrid Classical-Quantum Architecture as the Practical Bridge:
   - Level 1 (Classical): Use ISIS3 / GDAL for orthorectification, tiling, and
     extracting candidate interest points via classical detectors (SIFT/RIFT).
   - Level 2 (Quantum): Feed the candidate correspondence matrix (e.g. 50-100
     keypoints) into a QPU solving the QUBO graph matching problem to eliminate
     outliers with global optimality.
   - Level 3 (Classical): Run sub-pixel refinement and final homography/bundle
     adjustment.
